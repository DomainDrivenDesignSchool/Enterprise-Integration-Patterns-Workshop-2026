# 🟢 Group 3 - The City Planners

> **Cities are not defined by their buildings. They are defined by the movement they enable.**

---

# Mission

The Messengers discovered **what** moves.

The Flow Architects discovered **how** it moves.

Your responsibility is to discover **where** those flows live.

You are not designing a new architecture.

You are trying to understand the architecture that already exists.

Think like an urban planner.

Observe before proposing solutions.

---

# Input

Use the following artifacts.

- Enterprise Message Catalog
- Enterprise Message Flow

These are your primary inputs.

---

# Task 1 - Draw the Enterprise Landscape

Draw every major participant involved in the business process.

Examples include:

- Applications
- Services
- External Organizations
- Legacy Systems
- Humans
- Vendors
- Government Services

Do not think about deployment.

Think about business capabilities.

Your landscape should answer one question.

> Who participates in this business?

---

# Task 2 - Connect the Landscape

Now place the Message Flow on top of your landscape.

For every interaction identify:

- Which systems communicate?
- Which messages travel?
- How do they communicate?

Examples:

- REST
- Messaging
- Shared Database
- Batch
- File Transfer
- Manual
- Email

Do not optimise anything.

Just observe reality.

---

# Task 3 - Find the City Bottlenecks

Imagine this architecture as a city.

Ask yourselves:

Where would traffic jams appear?

Where does everything depend on one road?

Which intersections look dangerous?

Where would an accident affect the largest part of the city?

Mark every observation.

Do not propose solutions.

---

# Task 4 - Critical Infrastructure

Every city has critical infrastructure.

Identify the following.

## Critical Roads

Which communication paths carry the largest amount of business value?

---

## Critical Buildings

Which systems are essential for the business to continue operating?

---

## Single Points of Failure

Which single component worries you the most?

Why?

---

## Invisible Infrastructure

What important dependencies are NOT visible in diagrams?

Examples:

- Shared databases
- Human approvals
- Scheduled jobs
- Excel files
- Manual phone calls
- Tribal knowledge

---

# Task 5 - Future Growth

Now imagine the business doubles in size next year.

Without redesigning anything, discuss:

- Where would congestion appear first?
- Which communication style would become problematic?
- Which systems would struggle?
- Which dependencies would become dangerous?
- Which part of the city looks impossible to evolve?

Document your reasoning.

---

# Final Deliverable

Produce one artifact.

# Enterprise Integration Landscape

Your map should answer one question.

> How does the enterprise enable, or prevent, the movement of information?

Bring your observations.

Not your solutions.

The workshop is where we will discover why Enterprise Integration Patterns exist.
